Where people actually land

Pocatello isn't one market — it's a valley with a bench on each side, a university in the middle, and a ring of small towns that each solve a different problem. Here's how I'd sort them if you were sitting across from me.

Here’s the part that surprises people moving in from a bigger market: the price per square foot barely moves between $140,000 and $600,000. It sits right around $180 the whole way up. In Pocatello, paying more mostly buys you more house — not a different class of house. It only starts climbing past $700,000.

Before you get excited about a five–figure price

Every Pocatello listing I found under $100,000 was a manufactured or mobile home in a park — usually 540 to 1,000 square feet, with lot rent owed monthly on top of the purchase price. They finance differently than a stick–built house, so talk to a lender before you fall in love with one. The least expensive conventional house I found was $139,900, and there were only eleven listings in the entire city under $200,000. That’s why the honest number to budget is closer to $200,000.

Highland

The east-bench suburbia most out-of-state buyers picture when they picture Pocatello: newer subdivisions climbing the hillside, wide streets, garages, valley views out the back. Resale demand here has historically been the steadiest in town — ask me for the numbers behind that.

FitsRemote-salary buyers, and anyone who wants turnkey and doesn't want to renovate.

University

The numbered avenues around Idaho State — older homes, a good share of them cut into rentals, and the shortest walk to campus in the city. Inventory is thin here and it moves on the university’s calendar more than the market’s, so what is for sale in March looks nothing like August.

FitsInvestors who understand student rentals, staff and faculty, anyone who wants to walk to work.
Original townsite & west side$200k–$340k

Historic Downtown Pocatello

The oldest part of the city and the most architecturally interesting. Craftsman and mid-century homes on the original townsite, brick storefronts a few blocks off, and the west-side streets running out toward the bench with real sunset views. It is also where the valley’s most affordable conventional houses are. Condition varies block to block more than anywhere else in town — walk it before you fall in love with a listing photo.

FitsCharacter-home buyers, first-time buyers, walkability people, anyone whose budget is the constraint.
North of the gap$300k–$460k

North Pocatello

Single-family neighborhoods and mobile parks on the flatter north end, closest to the industrial corridor and the fastest run to the freeway. Practical, unpretentious, and priced accordingly. Check which way the prevailing wind runs on any given street — more on that in the honest section.

FitsShift workers, commuters to Chubbuck or Fort Hall, budget-first buyers.

South Pocatello

Scenic hillside and riverside pockets heading toward the Portneuf Gap, plus rural acreage once you're past the last subdivision. Quieter, greener, and the shortest drive to Mink Creek and the ski hill. Lots get large fast out here.

Buckskin

Secluded mountain properties southeast of town for people who genuinely want to be away from it all. Trees, privacy, and a driveway you will think hard about every January. Not a starter area, and not a place to buy without asking about the road, the water and the power.

Its own city$330k–$560k

Chubbuck

Technically a separate city, functionally the north end of the metro — and where most of the new construction has gone. Single-family subdivisions, townhomes and condos, mobile parks and rural acreage all inside a few square miles, with the newest retail and the shortest grocery run in the valley.

FitsNew-construction buyers, and anyone who wants flat streets and sidewalks.
Within an hour
50 min north

Idaho Falls

The bigger city on the Snake River, and the reason a lot of Southeast Idaho paychecks exist — Idaho National Laboratory buses run from all over the region. More retail, more restaurants, more flights, higher prices than Pocatello.

FitsINL employees, buyers who want a bigger-city feel with a Yellowstone-side location.
25 min north

Blackfoot

Farm-country county seat between the two cities, cheaper than either, and a legitimate split-the-difference if one of you works in Pocatello and the other in Idaho Falls. Small-town pace, and yes, the potato museum is real.

FitsTwo-commute households, buyers priced out of both cities.
30 min west

American Falls

Reservoir town on the Snake, built around water — boating, fishing, and some of the best sunsets in the region. Small school district, small downtown, and the lowest entry prices of anywhere in this guide.

FitsWater people, and anyone whose commute is flexible.
15–40 min southeast

Inkom & Lava Hot Springs

The canyon towns. Inkom is a genuine 15-minute commute to Pocatello with a small-town elementary school; Lava is the hot springs resort town further up, where a share of the housing stock is short-term rental. Both put you at the base of the mountains.

FitsShort-commute rural buyers, and short-term rental investors in Lava specifically.

Become an Idahoan on paper

The Idaho specifics, in one place

Deadline
30 days from your move for both the driver's license and vehicle title/registration. Students at Idaho colleges and active-duty military are exempt.
Bring
Proof of lawful presence, identity, legal name, and Idaho residency — plus the current title, current registration, lienholder info, and the vehicle for VIN inspection.
Insurance
Idaho minimums are $25,000 per person for injury, $50,000 per accident, and $15,000 property damage.
Plates
Roughly six weeks to arrive by mail. You can track them in the DMV online portal.
The honest part

Every agent will tell you about the trails and the low cost of living. Here's the other column. None of it has stopped me from raising my kids here, but you should hear it before you're unpacking boxes.

Winter inversion

Cold air settles in the valley for stretches of December through February and traps whatever's in it. You get grey, hazy weeks with degraded air quality while the bench and the mountains above you sit in sunshine. It's the single most common complaint from newcomers. Idaho DEQ publishes air quality readings for the valley, and I would look at a full winter of them before deciding what it means for your household.

Wages, not just prices

Housing is cheap relative to Utah, California or Washington — and local wages reflect that. If you're bringing a remote salary, the math is excellent. If you're job-hunting locally, run your actual offer against your actual payment before you commit, not the cost-of-living index.

Getting on a plane

Pocatello Regional is small and service is limited. Most people drive Idaho Falls for regional flights or make the roughly two-and-a-half-hour run to Salt Lake City for anything else. Factor that drive into any job that expects you to travel.

The wind and the smell

The valley funnels wind, and on certain days with the wind in the wrong direction you'll catch industrial and agricultural odor from the north end. It isn't constant and it isn't everywhere, but it's worth standing on the street of a north-end house on a windy afternoon before you write the offer.

Retail and dining ceiling

This is a metro of roughly 90,000 people. You will drive to Idaho Falls or Salt Lake for some shopping, some concerts and some medical specialists. The food scene is better than people expect — see the next section — but it is not Boise, and pretending otherwise would waste your time.

The student cycle

Idaho State drives a real rental cycle. Near campus, that means August scarcity, cheap summers, and a rental market that behaves nothing like the rest of town. Great if you're investing on purpose; annoying if you're trying to rent for six months while you shop.

Older housing stock

Big parts of the original townsite and the west side were built well before modern insulation, wiring and sewer lines. That's where the character is and where the value is, but budget for updates and never skip the inspection because a house shows well.

Where to hike

Eleven trails worth knowing, roughly easiest first. All of them are inside a half-hour drive, and the first few start close enough to town that you can do them before work.

Edson Fichter loop

FLAT · ~1 MI · RIVERSIDE

Barely a hike, and that's the point — it's the one you do your first weekend with a stroller or a dog. Pond, ducks, a stocked fishing hole, and a rope swing the kids will find before you do.

City Creek

EASY–MODERATE · IN TOWN

The one everybody starts with. Trailhead minutes from downtown, forgiving grade, and wildflowers in late spring worth the trip on their own. Crowded on Saturdays, which tells you what locals think of it.

Red Hill to the Pillars

SHORT · STEEP · SUNRISE

Straight up the hill above the university. It's over quickly and it earns its reputation at either end of the day — this is the view of the valley people put on their wall. Also counts toward Petite Peaks.

Corral Creek

MODERATE · CREEKSIDE

Follows the water, gentle for the first stretch, well maintained. A good second hike once City Creek stops feeling like a workout.

Gibson Jack

MODERATE · SHADED

Creek the whole way and real tree cover, which makes it the August answer when everything else is baking. Keeps going if you want it to — see Elk Meadow below.

Cusick Creek

MODERATE

Opens in grassland and climbs into shaded forest, steady the whole way with some rocky stretches. Connects into the network heading toward Kinport.

Kinport Peak

MODERATE–HARD · VIA CITY CREEK

Up the Sap Tree Loop to the radio towers. You'll see them from town for months before you stand next to them, and the whole valley opens up underneath you when you do.

Chinese Peak

2.9 MI · 1,719 FT GAIN · 6,791 FT

Dirt road from the gate at the top of Barton, steady grade almost the whole way, no navigation required. The last half mile faces north and holds snow into May — plan around that, not through it.

Scout Mountain

MODERATE–HARD · MINK CREEK

Further out and quieter for it. Campsites up top if you want to make a night of it, and the kind of sunset that explains why people put up with February here.

Elk Meadow via Gibson Jack

12.9 MI · LOOP AT THE TOP

The big day. Three rocky pitches on the way up and a winter warming hut waiting at the end. Start early, bring lunch, and tell someone where you went.

Mountaintop Road

~5 HRS · NO SHADE

Desert floor up into pine, steep in places, and genuinely exposed once you're on the road itself. Carry more water than you think you need — this is the one that humbles people.
